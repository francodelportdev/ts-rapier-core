Push the boxes off the platform

A demo of @francodelportdev/rapier-engine, a 2D engine built on Rapier physics.

Click to play
  • ← → Move
  • Space Jump
  • E Kick the first box upwards

How it's built

  1. Create the game

    await Game.create(canvas) loads Rapier, which is WebAssembly, and then creates the physics world. That's why it's async and you can't use new Game().

  2. Add the entities

    Each entity creates its own body in the physics world. addEntity() adds it to the game loop so that it gets updated and drawn. Units are meters, with the Y axis pointing up and the origin at the center of the canvas.

    EntityBody typeBehavior
    Ground / PlatformfixedNever moves.
    BoxdynamicAffected by gravity, collisions and impulses.
    PlayerkinematicMoved by code. It pushes the boxes, and nothing pushes it.

  3. Add your own controls

    box.applyImpulse(0, 5) changes the box's velocity instantly. The box weighs 1 kg, so an impulse of 5 N·s adds 5 m/s upwards.

  4. Start the loop

    game.start() runs physics at a fixed 60 steps per second, whatever your screen's refresh rate. Each step calls update() on every entity and then world.step(). Each frame then calls draw().

Full source: src/main.ts

To use it in your own project, run bun add @francodelportdev/rapier-engine, add <canvas id="game"> to your HTML, and paste this code.

Player

Move & jump

Player is a platformer character. Rapier's kinematic character controller handles collisions: it detects the ground, steps over small bumps and pushes dynamic bodies out of the way.

Click to play
  • ← → or A D Move
  • Space, ↑ or W Jump (hold to jump higher)

How it works

  1. Acceleration, not instant speed

    Each step, the horizontal speed moves towards speed at acceleration m/s², and back to 0 at deceleration m/s². In the air, both are multiplied by airControl, so you can steer a jump without stopping dead.

  2. Gravity and jump

    A kinematic body ignores the world's gravity, so the player applies its own (gravity, 40 m/s² by default, for snappy jumps). The jump speed is √(2 · gravity · jumpHeight), which is exactly the speed needed to reach jumpHeight.

  3. Variable height

    If you release the key while still rising, the upward speed is multiplied by jumpCut once. A tap gives a short hop, and holding the key gives the full jump.

  4. Coyote time and jump buffer

    Two small timers make jumping feel fair. Coyote time lets you jump for coyoteTime seconds after walking off a ledge. The jump buffer remembers a press for jumpBuffer seconds, so pressing just before landing still jumps.

Player

Double jump

Set maxJumps: 2 and the player can jump once more in mid-air. The platform here is too high for a single jump.

Click to play
  • ← → Move
  • Space Jump, then Space again in the air

How it works

  • maxJumps is the number of jumps available before landing. 1 is a normal jump, 2 is a double jump, and 3 or more lets you keep jumping.
  • Every jump in the air resets the upward speed to the full jump speed, so it reaches the same height as a jump from the ground.
  • Walking off a ledge without jumping uses up the ground jump, so you only get the air jumps. Within coyote time, a jump still counts as a ground jump.
  • Landing restores every jump.

Dash

A short horizontal burst in the direction the player is facing. The gap here is too wide for a jump, so jump and then dash in the air.

Click to play
  • ← → Move
  • Space Jump
  • Shift or X Dash

How it works

  • The dash is off by default. Turn it on with dash: true to use the defaults, or pass an object to tune it.
  • During the dash, the horizontal speed is fixed at dash.speed and gravity is paused, so the player moves in a straight line. When it ends, the player continues at normal top speed.
  • You can dash any time on the ground, and once per jump in the air. cooldown sets the minimum time between dashes.
  • The player turns lighter green while dashing, and its eye shows which way it's facing.
OptionDefaultDescription
dash.speed20m/s during the dash.
dash.duration0.15Seconds. The distance covered is speed × duration, 3 m by default.
dash.cooldown0.4Seconds before the next dash.

Wall jump

In the air, push into a wall to slide down it slowly, then jump off to the other side. Climb the shaft, and at the top, wall jump off the left wall and double jump over the right one to reach the gold platform.

Click to play
0.0 m
  • ← → Move, and push into a wall to slide
  • Space Jump, or wall jump while sliding. Press again in the air to double jump

How it works

  1. Wall slide

    Each step, the player checks for a wall right beside it, using a thin probe 0.05 m wide. If it's in the air, falling and pushing towards the wall, it slides, and its fall speed is capped at slideSpeed. Walls are fixed or kinematic bodies, so boxes don't count.

  2. Wall jump

    Jumping while sliding sends the player up (jumpHeight) and away from the wall (pushSpeed). For controlLock seconds, the arrow keys are ignored. Without that, holding towards the wall would steer straight back into it, and you could climb a single wall.

  3. Wall coyote time

    For coyoteTime seconds after letting go of a wall, a jump is still a wall jump. This is the same idea as the ledge coyote time.

  4. Works with the rest

    A wall jump counts as the ground jump, so with maxJumps: 2 you can still double jump afterwards. A dash ends a slide. The state is "wallSlide" while sliding. The events are wallSlideStart and wallSlideEnd, with the wall's side, and jump with wallJump: true (see State & events).

Options

Off by default. Turn it on with wallJump: true for the defaults, or pass an object to tune it.

OptionDefaultDescription
wallJump.slideSpeed3Fastest fall while sliding, m/s.
wallJump.pushSpeed10Horizontal speed away from the wall, m/s.
wallJump.jumpHeightthe player's jumpHeightMeters.
wallJump.controlLock0.15Seconds without arrow keys after a wall jump.
wallJump.coyoteTime0.1Seconds after leaving a wall that a wall jump still works.

Player

One-way platforms

You jump up through them from below and stand on them from above. Hold ↓ and jump to drop through. Climb the tower, then compare with the solid platform on the right, which blocks you from below.

Click to play
on solid ground
  • ← → Move
  • Space Jump up through a platform
  • ↓ + Space Drop through

How it works

  1. Mark a platform as one-way

    Add oneWay: true to a Platform, or to any fixed Shape, including tilted ones. One-way platforms are drawn in a lighter gray, and a Platform also gets a bright top edge.

  2. Solid only from above

    For the player, a one-way platform is solid only when the player's bottom edge is above the platform's top surface, at the left side, the center or the right side of the player. Coming from below or the side, the player passes through. One-way platforms are never walls for wall slides.

  3. Drop through

    Pressing jump while holding ↓ (keys.down) on a one-way platform drops through it instead of jumping. For dropThroughTime seconds (0.25 by default), every one-way platform is ignored, so the player doesn't land back on the same one. Dropping uses up the ground jump, just like walking off a ledge. The dropThrough event fires.

Only the player passes through. Other bodies, such as boxes or dynamic shapes, always collide with one-way platforms. player.isOnOneWayPlatform() tells you whether ↓ + jump would drop through.

State & events

The player reports what it's doing, so your game can play sounds, switch animations or update the UI without changing the engine.

Click to play
idle
    • ← → Move
    • Space Jump (twice for a double jump)
    • Shift Dash
    • R Respawn

    State

    player.getState() returns one of these values. It's updated once per physics step.

    StateWhen
    "idle"On the ground and not moving.
    "run"On the ground and moving.
    "jump"In the air, going up.
    "fall"In the air, going down.
    "dash"During a dash.
    "wallSlide"Sliding down a wall (see Wall jump).

    Very short losses of ground contact, under 0.05 s (for example when spawning or going over a bump), don't count as "fall". Without this, the state would flicker. Other getters: isGrounded(), isDashing(), getVelocity() (m/s) and getFacing() (1 or -1).

    Events

    player.on(event, listener) subscribes and returns a function that unsubscribes. There's also once() and off(). The payloads are fully typed.

    EventPayloadWhen
    stateChange{ from, to }The state changed.
    jump{ airJump, wallJump, jumpNumber }A jump started. airJump is true for extra jumps in the air, and wallJump for jumps off a wall.
    land{ fallSpeed }The player touched the ground after being in the air. Useful for dust particles or fall damage.
    dash{ direction, inAir }A dash started.
    dropThrough{}The player dropped through a one-way platform (see One-way platforms).
    wallSlideStart / wallSlideEnd{ side }Started or stopped sliding down a wall. side is 1 for a wall on the right and -1 for one on the left.
    respawn{ fell }The player went back to its start position. fell is true if it fell below respawnBelow, and false if you called respawn().
     
    const off = player.on("land", ({ fallSpeed }) => {
  if (fallSpeed > 15) shakeCamera();
});

off(); // stop listening

    Listeners run synchronously, inside the physics step. Keep them short, and do heavy work (such as loading assets) elsewhere.

    Engine

    Removing entities

    Rapier keeps simulating every body in the world, even when it's off screen. Removing entities you no longer need frees their CPU time and memory.

    Click to play
    0 bodies 0 removed
    • ← → Push boxes off the edge
    • C Clear every box

    How it works

    1. Kill plane (automatic)

      Any entity whose center falls below killPlaneY is removed automatically. The default is -30 m, well below the screen. Set it with Game.create(canvas, { killPlaneY: -30 }), or pass null to turn it off. The player respawns at -15 m (respawnBelow), so it never reaches the kill plane.

    2. Remove it yourself

      game.removeEntity(entity) takes the entity out of the game loop and calls entity.destroy(), which removes its body and colliders from the Rapier world. The player also frees its character controller. You can call it from anywhere, even inside update() or an event listener: the removal waits until the current physics step ends. Removing the same entity twice has no effect.

    3. Find out what was removed

      game.on("entityRemoved", ({ entity, reason }) => ...) runs after each removal. reason is "killPlane" or "manual". Use it to update a score, spawn a replacement, or clean up your own references to the entity.

    A destroyed entity can't be added again, so create a new one instead. game.getBodyCount() returns how many bodies the physics world has, which is handy to confirm that nothing leaks.

    Source: src/demos/remove.ts

    Camera

    Every game has a game.camera. It decides which part of the world appears on the canvas, so levels can be wider than the screen.

    Click to play
    camera
    • ← → Move
    • Space Jump (×2)
    • Shift Dash
    • - = Zoom

    How it works

    1. Follow

      camera.follow(player, options) moves the view towards the player every physics step. With smoothing, it covers about 63% of the remaining distance in smoothing seconds, so it eases in without overshooting. Inside the dead zone, a box around the center of the screen, the player moves freely without dragging the view, which avoids constant small jitter.

    2. Bounds

      camera.setBounds({ minX, maxX, minY, maxY }) keeps the view inside the level, so the camera stops at the edges instead of showing empty space. If the level is smaller than the screen on an axis, the camera centers on it.

    3. Zoom and shake

      camera.zoom scales everything, where 1 is 30 px per meter. camera.shake(intensity, duration) jolts the view by up to intensity meters and fades out. This demo shakes on hard landings, using the player's land event.

    4. Entities don't change

      The game passes the camera's scale, centerX and centerY to draw(), so the usual px = centerX + x · scale already accounts for the camera. Your own entities work with it as they are.

    API

    MemberDescription
    follow(target, { smoothing, deadZone, offset })Follow an entity, or pass null to stop. Defaults: 0.15 s, 2 × 1.5 m and (0, 0).
    setBounds(bounds | null)Keep the view inside a rectangle, in meters.
    x, y, zoomThe world point at the center of the canvas, and the zoom. You can read and write them.
    moveTo(x, y)Jump to a point, without smoothing.
    snapToTarget()Jump to the followed entity, for example after a respawn.
    shake(intensity, duration = 0.3)Shake by up to intensity meters, fading out.
    screenToWorld(px, py) / worldToScreen(x, y)Convert between canvas pixels and world meters, for example for mouse input.
    getVisibleBounds()The part of the world on screen, in meters.

    If the camera stops following an entity because it was removed, the camera stays where it is. Game.create(canvas, { grid: true }) draws a 1 m grid in world space, which makes the camera's movement visible.

    Source: src/demos/camera.ts

    Geometry

    Shape is a physics body with any geometry: boxes, circles, capsules, ramps, convex polygons and polylines. Use fixed shapes to build levels, and dynamic ones for objects that fall, roll and bounce.

    Click to play
    click to drop
    • ← → Move (walk up the ramp, try the steep slab)
    • Space Jump (×2)
    • Click to drop a shape
    • 1–5 Choose circle, box, triangle, hexagon or capsule

    Geometry

    Sizes are in meters. Points are relative to the shape's position.

    geometryDescription
    { type: "box", width, height }A rectangle, centered on the position.
    { type: "circle", radius }A circle. A line from its center shows it rolling.
    { type: "capsule", radius, height }A vertical segment of height with half-circles at both ends.
    { type: "ramp", width, height, rises? }A right triangle whose bottom edge is centered on the position. rises is "right" (the default) or "left".
    { type: "polygon", points }A convex polygon. If the points aren't convex, Rapier uses their convex hull, the smallest convex shape around them. The drawing shows the hull that Rapier actually built.
    { type: "polyline", points }Connected line segments for terrain, such as the hills here. A polyline can be concave, but it has no inside, so it can't be dynamic.

    Options

    OptionDefaultDescription
    x, yrequiredPosition, in meters.
    angle0Rotation in radians, counterclockwise.
    body"fixed""fixed" never moves, "dynamic" falls and collides, and "kinematic" moves only from code.
    density1Mass per square meter. A heavier shape pushes lighter ones harder.
    friction0.50 is ice and 1 is rubber.
    restitution0Bounciness. 1 bounces back to the same height. When two surfaces touch, the bouncier one wins.
    colorby body typeGray for fixed, orange for dynamic and blue for kinematic.

    Methods: applyImpulse(x, y), applyTorqueImpulse(torque) to spin it, and getAngle().

    The player on slopes

    The player walks up and stands on slopes up to maxSlope, which defaults to 50°. It doesn't slide down them while standing still. Anything steeper counts as a wall: the player can't stand on it or jump up it, and with wall jump enabled, it can wall slide on it.
